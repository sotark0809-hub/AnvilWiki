## How do skill trees work in Enshrouded?

Enshrouded has no locked classes: every character can invest in the same 12 branch skill trees plus a central pool of shared skills, color-coded into melee (red), survival and ranged (green), and magic (blue). Skill points come from leveling up—capped at level 50 in 1.0—and from destroying Shroud Roots out in the world.

| Color | Skill trees | Typical playstyle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Tank, Warrior, Barbarian, Athlete | Melee damage, shields, survivability |
| Green | Survivor, Beastmaster, Ranger, Assassin | Bows, daggers, pets, exploration perks |
| Blue | Trickster, Wizard, Healer, Battlemage | Wands, staves, spells, support |

Four structural rules matter before you spend a single point:

- **Respec is cheap and repeatable.** You can reset your skill points at any Flame Altar by spending Runes, so an early "mistake" is never permanent.
- **Runes do more than respec.** They are also reportedly used to upgrade armor, shields, and rings, which is why community guides warn against burning them all early.
- **Skill points are finite.** The exact total at the new level 50 cap is [TBC]; in the Update 7 era (level cap 45), characters had 184 points to distribute.
- **1.0 wiped every skill tree.** The official 1.0 changelog confirms all skill trees were reset when 1.0 launched—"hopefully for the last time," in the developers' own words. Veteran or not, plan your route before leaving the altar.

## What did Update 8 and 1.0 change about combat?

Two patches define today's build meta: Update 8 ("Forging the Path", April 2026) rebuilt combat fundamentals, and the 1.0 launch stacked new spells and skills on top. The headline changes:

- **Focus fuels weapon Special Abilities.** Focus is a dedicated resource that powers a special ability—an ultimate—for each weapon type.
- **Heavy attacks deal double damage** and build up an enemy's stun bar, rewarding slow, deliberate melee.
- **The armor damage-reduction cap rose from 60% to 80%**, which reportedly brought heavy armor back into the meta.
- **Nine new spells arrived in 1.0**, including Summon Fire, Ice, and Shock Totem, Summon Hollow Skeleton, Mana Drain, Entangle, Pillar of Flames, Blizzard, and Ball Lightning.
- **Build-defining new skills** include Advanced Necromancy, Totem Mastery, and Blast and Cast for mage-style builds, plus Puncture, Barrage, and Survival Instinct for ranger-style builds.
- **Eternal spells got a second life:** there are now 22 Tier 7 Eternal spells, and Eternal spells and arrows can be upgraded with perks.

## How do you play each of the nine builds?

Each breakdown below covers the loadout community guides converge on for 1.0: core weapons, stat priorities, signature skills, armor, and the moment-to-moment play loop. For how the nine compare head-to-head, see our [tier list](/guides/enshrouded-tier-list/).

### Ranger (Bow + Daggers)

The Ranger is reportedly the strongest build in 1.0 according to expertgamereviews. Open fights from range with bow skills, then finish closed gaps with daggers. Community skill picks include Multi-shot, Ricochet, Chain Reaction, Fatal Precision, Marksman, Eagle Eye, and Shell Shock. The bow tree took a nerf when it was reworked during Early Access, but guides report it recovered in 1.0 after Eternal arrows switched to mana costs. Pair it with the Quetzal Hunter armor set and a Ghost Glider for mobility.

### Battlemage (Wand & Shield + Staff)

Hack the Minotaur's #1 pick. Wands reportedly top the damage-efficiency charts because they regenerate mana passively, and a staff layered on top adds burst. Take Intelligence and Constitution, the Flaming Gauntlets, and skills like Unity, Arcane Deflection, Wand Specialization, plus the Aerial and Evasion Wand Attacks—and 1.0's new Blast and Cast combo. The loop: hold the shield line, chip with wand fire, unload staff nukes, and save your weapon special ability for staggered targets.

### Barbarian (Two-Handed Greatsword)

A Strength-based melee build that benefits directly from Update 8: heavy attacks hit for double damage and fill the stun bar, while the 80% mitigation cap makes heavy armor like the Sunpiercer set genuinely viable. Stagger enemies with heavy attacks, then cash in your weapon special ability while they are reeling.

### Wizard (Staff + Wand)

The classic glass cannon—and reportedly the squishiest build in the game. The staff's lack of a dedicated Special Ability is a known weakness. Community skill picks include Quick Charge, Mass Destruction, Chain Hit, Terror, Radiant Aura, and Blink. Scale Intelligence and Spirit, keep your distance, blink out of trouble, and delete packs before they close in.

### Summoner (New in 1.0)

The headline new playstyle of the 1.0 launch. Advanced Necromancy lets you raise Hollow Skeletons, while Totem Mastery powers the new Fire, Ice, and Shock totem summons. Community guides report it as a strong new archetype rather than a gimmick. Deploy totems at the edge of a fight, let skeletons hold aggro, and add your own damage from safety.

### Assassin (Daggers + Bow)

High ceiling, high effort. Daggers demand positioning and timing, per community consensus, and the Bloodfeather armor set supports the hit-and-run style. Open with bow shots, dash in for dagger combos, then disengage before a trade goes bad. Less forgiving than the Ranger, which shares its Dexterity scaling.

### Warrior (One-Handed + Shield)

The dependable all-rounder: a one-handed weapon and shield for survivability, Wolf's Fang as the community armor pick, and skills like Battle Heal, Veteran, and Blood Rage. Reportedly the most beginner-friendly build in the game—forgiving to play, simple to gear, and strong enough to carry you through the campaign.

### Tank / Guardian (Sword and Board)

Built for groups rather than solo speed. The Golden Bulwark set anchors the frontline, with community skill picks like Merciless Attack, Nemesis, and Thick Skin, and the raised 80% mitigation cap helps the fantasy land. In solo play, its modest damage holds it back.

### Healer (Wand + Support Spells)

The co-op specialist in Waveborne Ritualist gear. Solo leveling is slow, but in a full group a dedicated healer keeps melee fighters alive through the late-game regions and boss encounters, where damage checks get serious.

## Which build should beginners pick first?

The Warrior is reportedly the most beginner-friendly choice: shield blocking absorbs mistakes, Battle Heal covers downtime, and nothing in the kit demands tight execution. If you prefer fighting at range, the Ranger is the other common starter suggestion. One 1.0-specific warning from the official changelog: since every skill tree was reset, plan your points before heading out—and keep Runes for gear upgrades rather than impulse respecs. ## FAQ

### Can you respec in Enshrouded?

Yes. Visit any Flame Altar and spend Runes to reset your skill points, then rebuild freely across all 12 trees. respec costs mean experimentation stays affordable, but Runes also upgrade gear, so budget them.

### How many skill points do you get in Enshrouded 1.0?

The level cap is now 50, and the enshrouded.tools database puts the point total at max level at 212 — a database figure, since its build planner allocates exactly 212 points at the cap. For reference, the Update 7 era (level cap 45) gave characters 184 points, and Shroud Roots grant extra points on top of leveling.

### Did the 1.0 update reset skill trees?

Yes. The official 1.0 changelog confirms every skill tree was reset when 1.0 launched, with the developers hoping it is "the last time." Old characters keep their levels but re-allocate all points.

### What is the single best build in Enshrouded 1.0?

There is no official answer, and community sources disagree: expertgamereviews ranks the Ranger first, while Hack the Minotaur crowns the Battlemage. Both sit in S tier on our [tier list](/guides/enshrouded-tier-list/), and both are fully viable everywhere in 1.0.

### Is there a class system?

No locked classes exist. All 12 skill trees are open to every character, and since you can respec at Flame Altars with Runes, you can rebuild around any weapon whenever you find better gear.
