Last Updated: October 30, 2026 (Enshrouded 1.0, Hotfix 1.0.0.3)

Enshrouded hands you a sprawling voxel world and almost no instructions, so your first few hours decide whether you snowball into the mid-game or stall out gathering twigs. This beginner guide lays out a proven opening order for version 1.0, where to put your first base, how the Shroud timer rules actually work, and the early mistakes that cost new players the most time.

## What should you do first in Enshrouded?

Follow a fixed first-hour checklist: craft basic tools, place your first Flame Altar, set up a Workbench, rescue the Blacksmith as early as you can, start a Charcoal Kiln, and unlock the three mobility staples — the glider, the grappling hook, and double jump — before you explore anything risky. The order matters because each step unlocks the next: tools feed the altar, the altar anchors your base, the base houses survivors, and survivors unlock the crafting chains that make exploration survivable.

| Order | Do this | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish the tutorial, then gather wood, stone, twigs, and plant fiber | Nearly every early recipe draws on these four basics |
| 2 | Craft your first kit: axe, pickaxe, a weapon, a torch, and a shield | Tools gate all further gathering; a torch and shield keep early fights survivable |
| 3 | Place your first Flame Altar (built from 5 stones) | It is your respawn point, building zone, and a free fast-travel anchor; in 1.0 the first altar upgrade is free |
| 4 | Set up a Workbench | Lets you repair gear for free instead of crafting replacements |
| 5 | Build a small 3×3 shelter and stop there | You will relocate soon; overbuilding the starter camp is wasted effort |
| 6 | Rescue the Blacksmith, Oswald Anders — the Summoning Staff costs just one twig | He unlocks the core crafting progression: better tools, armor, and the kiln |
| 7 | Place a bed and shelter for him | Summoned survivors need a home before they can get to work |
| 8 | Build the Charcoal Kiln, then a Forge | The kiln has the longest processing timer in the early game — start it early and let it run in the background |
| 9 | Craft the Glider and Grappling Hook, and pick up the Double Jump skill | This mobility trio defines how you move across the entire map |
| 10 | Climb the Springlands Ancient Spire | These puzzle towers have no enemies; the summit reveals nearby points of interest and unlocks a fast-travel point |

With the Blacksmith freed, the kiln burning, and the spire climbed, you have everything you need to leave the tutorial bubble on your own terms — and the [Enshrouded map guide](/guides/enshrouded-map-guide/) becomes genuinely useful at exactly this point.

## Where should you build your first base?

Build your first base on flat, open ground outside the Shroud and inside your Flame Altar's radius — community guides most often point to the area around Harvest Homestead or the ground northeast of Mistbury Catacombs, reportedly the two most convenient early spots. Location matters more than decoration at this stage, for four reasons:

- **Stay outside the fog.** A camp beyond the Shroud's edge carries none of the timer pressure or Deadly Shroud risk.
- **Hug your altar.** The area inside a Flame Altar's radius is a safe zone, so cluster your key crafting stations inside it.
- **Keep it small and comfortable.** A 3×3 shelter is enough at first; spend surplus resources on comfort items instead, because Comfort feeds the Rested buff.
- **Plan to move.** The starter camp is temporary by design — do not sink hours into walls and decoration you will abandon within a day.

## Which difficulty settings should beginners choose?

Start on the default difficulty and adjust once you understand the survival loop. The game ships with a gentler casual-style preset, the default experience, a harder mode, a stricter survival mode, and a fully custom option, so you can tune the friction instead of suffering through it.

Two custom toggles come up constantly in community advice: set Loot Loss on Death so you keep everything, and switch Weapon Durability off. According to community guides such as Nerd Stash, those two changes remove the most punishing setbacks for new players without trivializing combat.

| Setting | Options | Beginner recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Difficulty preset | Casual-style / Default / Hard / Survival / Custom | Default to start; the casual preset if the survival loop feels overwhelming |
| Loot loss on death | Drop on death / keep all | Keep all (custom mode; community recommendation) |
| Weapon durability | On / Off | Off (custom mode; community recommendation) |

## How do you survive the Shroud?

Treat the Shroud like a dive with a limited air supply: check the timer color before you step in, plan your exit route first, and favor several short trips over one deep push. The timer drains while you stand inside the fog, and its color tells you how much trouble you are in.

| Shroud type | Timer behavior | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| Blue (standard) Shroud | Timer drains steadily while you remain inside | Leave before it empties; at zero you die |
| Red (Deadly) Shroud | Kills in moments regardless of your timer | Stay out entirely until far higher Flame levels — Shroud Removal unlocks at Flame Level 11 in 1.0 |
| Any Shroud, any time | Fast travel is disabled inside the fog | Plan your route in and out before you enter |

Beyond the timer: carry bandages, bring a ranged weapon for the flying enemies that patrol the fog, and remember that 1.0 nerfed the items that extend Shroud time while scattering more hourglass capsules around the world — the design now clearly rewards short, surgical trips.

## How should you spend Metal Scraps and Runes early?

Pour early Metal Scraps into upgrading your tools, not your weapons, and treat Runes as a long-game currency: spend only what your tools demand early, and bank the rest. Late-game equipment and skill respecialization both feed on Runes — you can reset your skill tree at a Flame Altar for a Rune cost — so every Rune wasted in hour one is a Rune you will miss at level 50.

The skill-tree point matters more than usual right now: 1.0 reset every player's tree, and the official changelog flatly encourages planning your build before you wander out of base.

## What are the most common beginner mistakes?

Most early deaths and wasted hours trace back to five avoidable habits.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Building a permanent base near the starting Cinder Vault | You outgrow the area quickly and abandon everything you built | Treat the first camp as disposable; relocate to open Springlands ground once the Blacksmith is free |
| Pushing deep into the Shroud before upgrading your Flame Altar | A thin safety net and a timer you cannot outrun | Climb the Flame levels on the boss chain first, then dive deeper |
| Lingering until the timer hits zero | Death, lost cargo, and a long walk back | Exit while the bar is still healthy; short trips compound |
| Spending Runes freely in the early hours | Late-game gear and respecs cost far more | Limit Runes to tool needs; bank the surplus |
| Leaving base right after the 1.0 skill reset without a plan | Early points get scattered across dead-end nodes | Pick a direction — melee, ranger, or mage — and commit your first points deliberately |

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play Enshrouded solo?

Yes. It is a co-op survival action RPG at heart, and you can play the entire game solo or invite up to 16 players into your world.

### What should I do right after the tutorial?

Gather wood, stone, twigs, and plant fiber; craft your first tools; place the Flame Altar; then beeline for the Blacksmith. After that: kiln, the mobility trio, and the Springlands Ancient Spire.

### Why can't I fast travel out of the Shroud?

Fast travel is disabled inside the Shroud by design. Always plan an exit route before you enter, and never push deeper than your timer allows.

### Should I save my Runes?

Mostly, yes. Spend the minimum your early tools need and bank the rest for late-game gear and skill resets at the Flame Altar.

### What changed for beginners in 1.0?

The full release reset every skill tree, made your first Flame Altar upgrade free, and layered in quality-of-life improvements like chest upgrades and nameable map markers.
